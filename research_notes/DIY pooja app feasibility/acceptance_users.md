# Religious/Cultural Acceptability and User Behavior: Self-Performed (App-Guided) Pooja

Research note (Oct 2026). Some fetches failed (hinduismtoday.com and hds.harvard.edu were unreachable), so a few claims rest on search-result excerpts and not on full pages. Much of the online content on puja procedure comes from pandit-booking companies (SmartPuja etc.), which have a commercial interest in saying a priest is needed. Those sources are flagged where they're used.

## 1. Shastric view: which rituals can a grihastha do alone vs. which need a pandit?

### Takeaway
Daily home worship and bhakti-style festival pujas (nitya puja, Lakshmi puja, Satyanarayan katha, Chalisa/Sunderkand paath) are widely accepted as things a household does without a priest. Elaborate Vedic, Agamic or samskara rites (vivah, antyeshti, shraddha with pinda, shanti and homa rites) traditionally involve a purohit, though the family member (son or yajman) remains the karta. Arya Samaj explicitly endorses lay-led havan and samskaras.

### Cited Findings
- Home rituals are "usually adopted as part of one's daily routine and performed without the expertise of a priest"; bhakti is "open to all Hindus regardless of their caste, life station or gender" — [SBS Cultural Atlas](https://culturalatlas.sbs.com.au/religions/hinduism/resources/hinduism-rituals-and-practices)
- A stricter view: Hinduism Today quotes the Karana Agama as saying only a well-qualified priest may perform both atmartha (self) and parartha (for others) puja. It also says the Shankaracharya advised that elaborate pujas require initiation, while for certain simple pujas initiation is not obligatory (search excerpt; full page unreachable) — [Hinduism Today](https://www.hinduismtoday.com/hpi/?p=3177)
- Popular guidance: "what is important is devotion not showy and elaborate rituals"; Hinduism gives the devotee freedom to perform pujas as they wish — [Hindu Blog](https://www.hindu-blog.com/2009/11/how-to-do-simple-hindu-puja-at-home.html)
- Satyanarayan puja: a forum answer describes families doing it at home with kalash, coconut, mango leaves and a deity picture. Purnima is customary but there are "no restrictions" on the day — [NoBroker forum](https://www.nobroker.in/forum/how-to-do-satyanarayan-puja-at-home-without-pandit-in-marathi/)
- Diwali Lakshmi puja: mainstream media and blogs publish step-by-step DIY vidhi (cleaning, kalash, Ganesh first, invocation, offerings, aarti). One says you "don't need to chant complex verses" — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/lifestyle/diwali-2025-lakshmi-puja-vidhi-step-by-step-ritual-guide); [Housiey](https://housiey.com/blogs/?p=5785)
- Even a pandit-booking site concedes that Lakshmi puja steps are "simple" but says mantra chanting is "complex" (commercial source). The same site says Udaka Shanti requires a pandit because of its intricate steps — [SmartPuja](https://www.smartpuja.com/blog/pandit-for-diwali-lakshmi-puja-in-jaipur/); [SmartPuja Udaka Shanti](https://www.smartpuja.com/blog/pandit-for-udaka-shanti-puja-in-lucknow/)
- Antyeshti: performed by "the eldest son of the deceased and the officiating priest" — [Britannica](https://www.britannica.com/topic/antyeshti)
- Shraddha: the son (or a substitute such as the husband, a nephew or a daughter's son, per Dharmasindhu) is the karta, and the family priest is consulted for the procedure. The karta gives dakshina to the brahmins who are fed and honoured — [Kamakoti / Dharmasindhu](https://kamakoti.org/kamakoti/dharmasindhu/bookview.php?chapnum=25); [Wikipedia Śrāddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Ar%C4%81ddha); [Advaita-l](https://lists.advaita-vedanta.org/archives/advaita-l/2012-November/033487.html)
- Daughters doing shraddha: modern writers argue it is permissible, while tradition prefers a qualified son if one exists — [Vedadhara](https://www.vedadhara.com/can-a-daughter-perform-shraddha-for-her-parents)
- Vivah (legal angle): Hindu Marriage Act s.7 requires the "customary rites" of either party, with saptapadi completing the marriage where it applies. The Act does not name a priest as an officiant. The Supreme Court has held that a marriage is invalid without the ceremonies actually being performed — [iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/section-7-of-hindu-marriage-act/); [Scroll](https://scroll.in/latest/1067352/rites-and-ceremonies-must-be-performed-for-hindu-marriage-to-be-valid-says-supreme-court)
- Arya Samaj: Ottawa's Hindu Vedic Sanstha held monthly havans for years "with no pundits or priests", with host families rotating. Its pioneers also did samskaras such as naming and funerals "in the Vedic way" — [ICCC Ottawa](https://icccottawa.org/havan). Dayananda Saraswati championed individuals' right to interpret the Vedas free of priestly control — [Careers360](https://www.careers360.com/question-who-founded-the-arya-samaj-and-pleaded-for-the-right-of-individuals-to-interpret-vedas-and-free-themselves-from-the-tyranny-of-priests-lnq). Arya Samaj schools teach students to perform sandhya and agnihotra themselves — [Advaita-l](https://advaita-vedanta.org/archives/advaita-l/2001-October/002034.html)

### Inferences
Proposed classification. These are synthesis and judgment calls. Only the items marked * have direct source support above.
- **DIY-friendly:** nitya puja*, Hanuman Chalisa paath, Sunderkand paath (recitation and bhakti texts with no initiation barrier), Diwali Lakshmi puja*, Satyanarayan katha*, Ganesh Chaturthi home sthapana (commonly done by households, but I found no specific source), Navratri kalash sthapana/ghatasthapana (same caveat), simple havan in the Arya Samaj style*.
- **Partially (DIY possible; many families prefer a pandit):** Rudrabhishek (Vedic Rudram chanting is traditionally tied to upanayana, but simple abhisheka with "Om Namah Shivaya" is common), Griha pravesh (vastu shanti/homa portion usually priest-led), Namkaran, Mundan, elaborate havan/homa.
- **Pandit-required in mainstream practice (app as a companion only):** Vivah*, Antyeshti*, Shraddha/Pind daan* (family karta plus priest), shanti rites like Udaka Shanti*.
- Product implication: position the app as the guide for a family-run puja in the DIY tier and as a preparation or companion tool (samagri, meanings, and pronunciation practice alongside a priest) in the other tiers.

### Gaps
- I couldn't fetch primary texts (Grhya Sutras, Dharmasindhu chapters on each samskara) for Namkaran, Mundan or Griha pravesh, so their tiering is inference.
- I found no source specific to Ganesh Chaturthi, Navratri kalash sthapana, Rudrabhishek or Sunderkand.

## 2. Sankalp, uchcharan and gotra; views of acharyas and pandits; e-puja controversy

### Takeaway
Traditional voices stress correct pronunciation, initiation (upanayana or diksha) for Vedic mantras, and priest training. Popular and bhakti voices stress intention over precision. Organised opposition targets online or proxy temple puja (fraud and priestly rights) more than family-run home puja.

### Cited Findings
- Hinduism Today: time spent mastering pronunciation is "time well spent"; ideally, training comes personally from a priest, pundit or Sanskrit-proficient person (search excerpt) — [Hinduism Today](https://www.hinduismtoday.com/hpi/?p=3177)
- Adhikara (eligibility) to recite Vedic mantras depends on upanayana in orthodox Smarta and Sri Vaishnava discussion. Women have traditionally not undergone upanayana, so orthodox views bar them from Vedic recitation including Gayatri. A Kanchi Mahaswami view against women chanting Gayatri is reported second-hand. Counter-voices cite Gargi and Maitreyi and smriti references to women once wearing the thread — [Advaita-l](https://advaita-vedanta.org/archives/advaita-l/2009-September/022520.html); [SriRangaSri](https://www.ibiblio.org/sripedia/srirangasri/archives/aug04/msg00068.html)
- A Reddit (r/hinduism) user says many Shakti practices require initiation, but "every single path has some practices that can be done by anyone without formal initiation" — [r/hinduism via redlib](https://redlib.hbubli.cc/u/TerminalLucidity_)
- Harvard Religious Literacy Project: online puja companies shift authority for religious decisions away from temple priests and toward the companies, and "often, temple priests do not agree with these instructions" (search excerpt; page unreachable) — [Harvard RLP](https://rpl.hds.harvard.edu/religion-context/case-studies/technology/internet-pujas)
- Kamakhya Devalaya (Guwahati) states it "never holds any online puja", takes no money for one, and threatened legal action against impersonators — [Sentinel Assam](https://sentinelassam.com/cities/guwahati-city/we-hold-no-online-pujas-kamakhya-devalaya-authority)
- Baba Vaidyanath (Deoghar) priests protested in 2003 when the temple went online, saying they were being deprived of their right to conduct rituals — [Religion.info](https://english.religion.info/2003/06/16/india-priests-sulk-as-jharkhand-temple-goes-online/)

### Inferences
- The highest-risk content is Vedic mantras (Rudram, Purusha Sukta, Gayatri, homa mantras). Offering Puranic or nama-mantra alternatives, plus audio pronunciation guidance, would cut orthodox objections.
- A sankalp template that inserts the user's name, gotra, place and tithi is a natural feature. Users who don't know their gotra commonly use a substitute; that practice is often cited as "Kashyap gotra", but I did not find a source in this research.
- Avoid claiming temple affiliation without written authorisation, given the Kamakhya precedent.

### Gaps
- I found no formal statement from the Shankaracharya mathas, ISKCON or another sampradaya specifically on app-guided home puja.
- I found no sourced material on gotra or sankalp requirements.

## 3. Regional and sampradaya variation

### Takeaway
The evidence is thin, but it points to stricter Agamic/Vedic gatekeeping in South Indian Smarta and Sri Vaishnava circles and in Shaiva Agama traditions. Arya Samaj, at the other pole, explicitly rejects priestly mediation. North Indian vrat-katha and bhakti practice is the most DIY-compatible.

### Cited Findings
- Karana Agama (Shaiva) restricts both atmartha and parartha puja to qualified priests — [Hinduism Today](https://www.hinduismtoday.com/hpi/?p=3177)
- Sri Vaishnava position: only those with upanayana may recite the Vedas — [SriRangaSri](https://www.ibiblio.org/sripedia/srirangasri/archives/aug04/msg00068.html)
- Arya Samaj runs lay-led havans and samskaras in the diaspora — [ICCC Ottawa](https://icccottawa.org/havan)
- Booking platforms segment pandits by community and language (Hindi, Kumaoni, North Indian, English-speaking), which shows that ritual style varies regionally — [SmartPuja Bangalore](https://www.smartpuja.com/blog/pandit-for-satyanarayan-puja-in-bangalore)

### Inferences
- The app needs region and sampradaya variants: Marathi Satyanarayan, Bengali Lakshmi puja on Kojagari, Tamil and Telugu vrata, and Gujarati Chopda pujan.

### Gaps
- I found no systematic study comparing North and South India on willingness to self-perform.

## 4. User behavior and demand

### Takeaway
Digital devotion is scaling fast (Sri Mandir at 3.5M MAU, with revenue nearly quadrupled in FY25). Diaspora users engage more deeply. Pandit cost and scarcity are real drivers. I found no independent survey that directly measures willingness to do pooja without a pandit.

### Cited Findings
- Sri Mandir (AppsForBharat): 3.5M monthly active users, more than 100 temples, over 30 countries, and about 20% of users outside India. In India, 20–25% of users both pray and make offerings in the app; in the US the overlap is about 50% (company-reported) — [TechCrunch, Jun 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/06/30/sri-mandir-keeps-investors-hooked-as-digital-devotion-grows)
- AppsForBharat FY25 operating revenue was ₹69.6 Cr (up from ₹18.5 Cr), with a net loss of ₹45.3 Cr — [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/appsforbharat-fy25-net-loss-widens-16-to-inr-45-cr)
- Sri Mandir Play reviews are positive, with users expressing pride in their Hindu identity. Complaints include pricing and requests for more Hindi content (third-party review summary) — [Kimola](https://kimola.com/reports/unlock-insights-with-sri-mandir-app-feedback-report-google-play-hi-in-154375)
- AstroSage's cofounder says much of faith-app user growth comes from Gen Z and millennials. Astrotalk's FY24 revenue reportedly doubled to ₹651 Cr — [Open Magazine](https://openthemagazine.com/cover-stories/faith-accompli)
- An unverified claim attributed to a 2023 YouGov-Mint survey: 53% of Gen Z Indians say religion is important, and 62% pray regularly "on their own terms". I couldn't locate the original — [SheThePeople](https://www.shethepeople.tv/author-spotlight/guest-contributions/gen-z-indias-gentle-shift-towards-faith-9537020)
- An unsourced claim: India's faith economy is about $58B (2023), and millennials aged 25–35 account for 70% of faith-tech revenue — [Kotak Securities](https://kotaksecurities.com/investing-guide/articles/digital-spirituality-india-faithtech-trends)
- Pandit fees (commercial listings): Satyanarayan with one pandit costs about ₹4,000–5,200 (₹9,000–16,000 for two pandits or premium), and Griha pravesh starts at ₹6,500 (₹5,100 for a rented home) — [SmartPuja Mumbai](https://www.smartpuja.com/blog/pandit-for-satyanarayan-puja-in-mumbai/); [SmartPuja service cards](https://www.smartpuja.com/blog/puja-service-cards). 99Pandit pricing "depends on puja, location and requirements" — [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/99pandit/pricing)
- Priest shortage: the priesthood is shrinking as members move to better-paying jobs or migrate abroad. In Tamil Nadu, priests charge more when demand peaks (2009–2012 reporting) — [Hinduism Today/HBL](https://www.hinduismtoday.com/hpi/?p=9724); [Hinduism Today/ABC](https://www.hinduismtoday.com/hpi/?p=11260)
- Diaspora: booking sites sell live-video Griha pravesh, citing a shortage of local priests abroad — [SmartPuja e-puja](https://www.smartpuja.com/blog/online-epuja-ceremony)

### Inferences
- The reasons people want DIY (inferred from the sources above): cost (₹4–16k per puja), pandit availability on peak muhurat days, diaspora shortage, wanting to understand the ritual, and the Gen Z/millennial pattern of praying "on their own terms".
- The reasons people avoid DIY: fear of doing it wrong (mantra precision), elders' expectations, and samskaras where a priest is socially or legally expected.
- The diaspora looks like the strongest early segment, given the higher US engagement on Sri Mandir.
- A hybrid model (DIY guide plus an optional pandit video call for the hard parts) would cover the "partially" tier.

### Gaps
- I found no independent survey on willingness to perform puja without a pandit.
- I found no 2025–26 independent data on pandit fees. The ₹1,100–₹21,000 range in the brief could not be verified beyond the commercial listings above.
- Reddit sentiment was only lightly sampled, because direct Reddit threads didn't surface in search.
